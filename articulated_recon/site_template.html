Articulated 4D reconstruction of the dynamic parts, from video plans

The robot is an articulated URDF model, the manipulated objects are box primitives, and the drawer is a primitive with a fitted prismatic joint. Each frame is fitted to the video by fast GPU optimisation. Multiview evidence comes from projecting the same 3D state into every camera and testing it against that camera's depth (z-buffer reasoning, no rasteriser in the loop). The ablations remove multiview, then depth and cameras. Every reconstruction is also rendered into all four cameras, so views not used for fitting are a held-out test.

Pipeline

  1. Lift: the depth and cameras give metric points per view and frame (ground truth, or predicted by the world model). A table plane is fitted with RANSAC, and the foreground is everything above it.
  2. Robot: the base pose (sliding the URDF's base column over the table) and then all 7 joints plus the gripper are optimised per frame from several starting points, then refined jointly. Loss: robust chamfer (observed to model), plus a multiview depth test on surface samples: on the surface (attract), in seen-empty space (penalise), occluded (no evidence).
  3. Objects by motion: points left after the robot are clustered. A frame-0 point is absent at a later frame if some camera now sees through it. Fully absent means a free rigid body (6-DoF pose per frame, hypotheses carried by the hand). Partly absent means an articulated object, split into a static parent and a moving child with a joint search (prismatic along the parent axes). Static clusters touched by moving parts are kept (the insertion target); everything else is dropped.
  4. Pixels only: text-prompted masks (Grounding-DINO + SAM2) are fitted by soft-silhouette render-and-compare (pytorch3d) with an unknown camera, a guessed 60° FOV, and metric scale from the URDF alone.

Time and how to make it faster

For 5 frames × 4 views at 512²: base search plus frame 0 (32 hypotheses) takes about 45 s, then about 7 s per further frame, a backward pass and joint refinement take 15–40 s, and the objects stage takes 25–60 s. That's about 2–3 min per clip on an H100 slice, not counting evaluation and rendering. Faster options:
  1. A calibrated rig (camera-to-base extrinsics, as RH20T provides) removes the base search. The world-model and real runs here already use this.
  2. Logged proprioception turns the robot fit into a check.
  3. Dense video makes this tracking instead of search: 1 warm start and about 50 iterations, roughly 0.1–0.3 s per frame. The multi-starts exist only because the package gives 5 sparse keyframes.
  4. 4× fewer surface samples, torch.compile, and all frames optimised in parallel.
  5. Moving the object stage's DBSCAN and loops to the GPU.
Pixels and 3D are complementary. The 3D (depth) term finds the right basin, and a silhouette or photometric term can sharpen the edges at the end. Pixels alone (last rows) fail at metric pose.
